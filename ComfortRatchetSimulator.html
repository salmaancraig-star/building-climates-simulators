Comfort Ratchet Simulator

How does the comfort ratchet narrow or broaden? Two people start work in the same Westwood office one September: an LA local whose indoor life has been air-conditioned to 22 ± 0.5 °C (71.6 ± 0.9 °F), and a newcomer from Medellín — La Eterna Primavera — whose indoor life has been naturally ventilated at 20–26 °C (68–79 °F) all year. The model follows their thermal expectations over days, weeks, months and years.

Year by year, the building, and how the model is built

Model based on Craig, Halepaska, Collins (2026), Thermal Comfort, Alliesthesia, and the Predictive Brain (pre-submission manuscript): eq. (sensation), (discomfort), (tolerance), (recursion-as-learning) and (alpha), with the constants below.

Brackets: the same year with every learning rate at the slow end of its range (α 0.10, β₁ 0.02, discomfort-weight relaxation 0.10 a year) or the fast end (0.40, 0.10, 0.44). “Accepts of what the standard accepts”: of the hours ASHRAE 55's adaptive 90 % envelope calls acceptable in this room, the share inside the newcomer's own comfort band. The standard's curve is not drawn: it is a pooled regression on outdoor air, and the gap between it and what either person expects is the building's doing.

The people. Each has an expectation P (where they expect the room to be) and a comfort band P ± w they accept. Sensation S = γS(T − P); discomfort D = γD|S|; the comfort band w = w₀ + c/(γSγD), with w₀ = 1.08 K and c = 1.01 fixed so that a lifetime at 22 ± 0.5 °C gives ±1.75 K (PMV ±0.5 at 3.5 K a vote — the 90 % envelope) and a settled naturally ventilated life gives ±2.50 K (ASHRAE 55's adaptive 90 % envelope). An hour is uncomfortable when the room is outside that day's comfort band — their own band, never the standard's. The newcomer arrives with the comfort band six years in the Medellín office under these constants give them (±2.31 K, P 23.0 °C); the local as a lifetime at the setpoint (±1.75 K, P 22 °C). The Medellín figure in the heading (19–26 °C) is the simulated office's own range over working hours (mean 22.7 °C, 90 % of hours 20.9–24.7 °C) with no heating or cooling at all; it is not the adaptive standard's band, which for Medellín's running mean of 19.7 °C would be 21.4–26.4 °C.

Three clocks. (1) P ← P + α(T̄ − P), α = γS/(γS + γP) = 0.20 a day — McCartney & Nicol's fitted optimum (c = 0.80; 90 % of a change in ten days), read with the room in place of the outdoor air; their fit is flat for c 0.33–0.90 and the constant was never fitted indoors, so α 0.10–0.40 is carried as a range. (2) Sensitivity γS is tuned to the lived spread of T − P: 1/γS ∝ s₀² + σ², σ² tracked at β₁ = 0.05 a day (90 % in about six weeks; habituation studies fix only the downward direction, for strong stimuli; 0.02–0.10 carried as a range). The floor s₀² is set so that a settled air-conditioned life is 1.5× as sensitive as a naturally ventilated one (Rupp et al. 2019 give ≈ 2, pooled-slope attenuation argues for less; 1.3–2 is the honest range). (3) The discomfort weight's level moves toward the regime's settled value — the two differ by 1.41 (Luo et al. 2018, demand factors 1.13/0.80) — by 0.9 of the gap a year when rising (Luo: confidence built “rapidly in the first year”) and 0.30 when falling (Luo's bound is 0.44; 0.10–0.44 carried as a range), × 1.5 with a window.

Three levels of control. A window lowers γD to 0.7 from the first day (Schweiker 2018: perceived control is worth about 2 K; the value is assumed) and speeds the slow clock × 1.5 (allostatic self-efficacy, Stephan et al. 2016; assumption). Clothing adds 2 K on the cool edge (a 0.25–0.36 clo sweater against ASHRAE 55's ≈ 3 K comfort-zone shift per 0.5 clo; rough) and comes with the windows as the second level of control. Ceiling fans add 2.2 K on the warm edge (ASHRAE 55 §5.4.2.4) as the third. None changes what they feel; all change what they accept. The comfort band is capped at 18 and 32 °C.

Energy and emissions. The running totals are per person (15 occupants) from the day of arrival to the end of the period shown: thermal cooling and heating from the building model divided by a seasonal COP of 3 (cooling, and heat-pump heating in both offices), times 0.22 kg CO₂e per kWh — LADWP's 2025 Power Content Label, 487 lb per MWh. In the naturally ventilated office the figure is what the air-conditioned office would have used, less this office's own supplementary heating. Both constants are rough and stated here so they can be changed.

The building and the weather. Top floor of a 1,000 m³ office (120 m² of north-heavy triple glazing, U 0.2 W/m²K, 50 MJ/K of mass), 15 people × 250 W, 10 L/s per person, weekdays 08–18. Naturally ventilated: windows open above 24 °C when it is cooler outside, night purge at 6 ACH above 22 °C, heating holds 18 °C in occupied hours. Air-conditioned: 22 ± 0.5 °C. Weather: Los Angeles Downtown (USC campus) TMYx 2009–2023 — the nearest typical-year file to Westwood (15 km; Westwood lies between it and Santa Monica). Medellín (Olaya Herrera, 1,506 m) for the newcomer's origin.

What is drawn. Two views of the same period. Overlaid: one chart with both people's bells, to watch the ratchet; separated: one chart per person on a shared scale, with the distributions of the hours too warm and too cool for that person. The axis is clipped to 12–32 °C (55–90 °F) so the indoor curves have room, which cuts the outdoor tails; the key sits to the right. Light blue: the outdoor temperature over all hours of the period, scaled to their working hours so that the areas compare. Grey: the room's temperature during the hours they are at work. Their bell is the sum of that period's daily bells (90 % of each day's area inside P ± w); the fill is the part inside each day's own comfort band, so it is exactly what they accept, and the thin curve beyond is the bell's shape, not their tolerance. Red and blue: the distribution of the hours that were too warm or too cool for them — counted hour by hour against that day's own edges, so they begin where the fill ends. The strip below is the ratchet itself: each person's comfort band half-width at every stop, on an axis spaced by the clocks.
